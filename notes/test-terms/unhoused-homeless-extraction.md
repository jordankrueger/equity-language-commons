## Source 1: SumOfUs 2016 — "A Progressive's Style Guide"

**File:** `source-guides/discovered/sumofus-progressive-styleguide-2016.pdf`
**Canonical URL:** None. Org (SumOfUs) merged into Ekō in 2022; guide is orphaned.
**Confidence:** VERIFIED-ARCHIVED

### Hit 1 — Economy section (p. 9)

Table column "Terms used / Terms avoided" under Economy issue area. Approved term:

> persons experiencing homelessness or illness

Listed alongside other approved economy terms (racial equity, economic opportunity, low-income as adjective).

**Note:** This appears in the Economy section, not Housing/Space — reflects SumOfUs framing homelessness as a class/poverty issue first, housing issue second. In our schema this is one guidance entry from the Economy page, separate from the Housing/Space page entry below.

### Hit 2 — Housing/Space section (p. 21)

Full writing-guidelines block begins:

> The United Nations' Universal Declaration of Human Rights defines housing as part of "the right to a standard of living adequate for the health and wellbeing of himself and his family." This approach centers people and access, not status and cost, and demands a public discourse that speaks to the universal, interdependent, and personal experience of housing. People-first language, as well as language that supports dignity and a broad understanding of housing and spatial injustice – housing discrimination, unaffordability, foreclosure and eviction, homelessness, etc. – are needed. Careful attention should be paid to ensuring that all people have an active voice in how they define their own housing situation.

Specific recommendation:

> Consider whether terms and phrasing are crass, inaccurate, or may reinforce stigma, implying criminalization or invoking fear (bum, indigent, vagrant, beggar) and take the time to re-word or frame the issue with adequate context to go against those patterns.

Y/? table on p. 21 ("Terms used by housing rights activists" / "Terms avoided/questioned by housing rights activists"):

| Used | Avoided/questioned |
|------|---------------------|
| favela | bum |
| ghetto (historical and current usages that illuminate injustices or belong to one's identity) | gentrification |
| green the ghetto | ghetto (as adjective or in context of hipster racism) |
| **homeless person** | **the homeless** |
| housing first | **transient** |
| slum (as self-definition) | — |
| workforce housing | — |

Sidebar quote cited in this section:

> "San Francisco represents a particularly important case of the criminalization of homelessness. Even in liberal San Francisco, the social construction of homelessness as bad behavior became powerful enough to propel large-scale police campaigns against nuisance offenses, repeated attempts to abolish general assistance, and numerous other programs aimed at pushing the 'visible poor' back into invisibility."
> — Teresa Gowan, *Steering city's homeless focus from sin to sickness*, San Francisco Public Press

### Fair-use quote to pull (< 50 words)

From the Specific Recommendations:

> Consider whether terms and phrasing are crass, inaccurate, or may reinforce stigma, implying criminalization or invoking fear (bum, indigent, vagrant, beggar) and take the time to re-word or frame the issue with adequate context to go against those patterns. (p. 21)

And from the Y/? table, use "homeless person" vs. "the homeless" / "transient" directly (short list, no 50-word concern).

### Synthesis note

SumOfUs 2016 recommends "homeless person" (identity-first, person-centered construction) as the approved term, alongside "persons experiencing homelessness" in the Economy section. The guide does **not** mention "unhoused" or "houseless" — the term hadn't entered mainstream progressive discourse yet. The guide's stance is anti-stigma and explicitly anti-criminalization: "bum, indigent, vagrant, beggar" are called out as stigmatizing or criminalizing, "the homeless" (definite-article aggregation) and "transient" are flagged.

Note the internal tension: the guide's two approved constructions (economy section "persons experiencing homelessness" vs. housing section "homeless person") are different grammatical approaches to the same principle (people-first) and the guide doesn't reconcile them. This is a 2016 artifact — the person-first vs. identity-first debate had not crystallized enough to drive style-guide authors to pick one.

## Source 2: Diversity Style Guide 2023 — glossary entry "homeless"

**File:** `source-guides/discovered/diversity-style-guide-2023-11.md`, lines 1786–1790
**Canonical URL:** https://www.diversitystyleguide.com/glossary/homeless-homelessness/
**Confidence:** VERIFIED (archived markdown matches live glossary URL; DSG is a living web document)
**Entry updated:** Not dated within DSG; `entry_updated` null pending clarification from DSG maintainer (same posture as Latinx DSG entry).

Full entry:

> An individual who lacks a fixed, regular and adequate nighttime residence. Some people prefer the term unhoused. When possible, ask which term people prefer. Use person-first language: people experiencing homelessness; avoid homeless people or the homeless.

### Fair-use quote (full entry, ~40 words — under limit)

> An individual who lacks a fixed, regular and adequate nighttime residence. Some people prefer the term unhoused. When possible, ask which term people prefer. Use person-first language: people experiencing homelessness; avoid homeless people or the homeless.

### Key moves in this entry

1. Uses the federal definitional boilerplate ("fixed, regular, adequate nighttime residence" — mirrors the McKinney-Vento Act language; DSG doesn't cite the source).
2. Introduces "unhoused" as a term **some people prefer**, without ranking it against "homeless." Neither is flagged as default.
3. Instructs self-ID deferral ("ask which term people prefer").
4. Takes a position on construction: **person-first preferred, identity-first avoided.** "People experiencing homelessness" in; "homeless people" and "the homeless" out.

The "avoid homeless people" instruction is stronger than SumOfUs's 2016 position, which approved "homeless person." This is the 2016→2023 shift that makes this term a time-capsule example of progressive style migration.

## Meta-framing (synthesis context, not guidance entries)

### NCDJ Disability Style Guide 2021 — person-first default retired

**File:** `source-guides/discovered/ncdj-disability-style-guide-2021.md`, line 92

> A note about person-first language. In the past, we have encouraged journalists and others to use person-first language ... as a default. Even with the caveat that this does not apply to all, we have heard from many people with disabilities who take issue with that advice. ... And so we are no longer offering advice regarding a default. Instead, we hope you will double down to find out how people would like to be described.

NCDJ's position is scoped to disability, but it names the same methodological shift that DSG's "ask which term people prefer" applies to unhoused/homeless. **Included as synthesis context. Not a guidance entry** — NCDJ doesn't define "homeless" or "unhoused."

## Schema stress-points encountered (for v0.3 consideration)

1. **Same-org multi-page guidance within one guide** (SumOfUs p. 9 Economy + p. 21 Housing) — schema v0.2 already allows this ("multiple entries per org"). Works correctly for this case. No change needed.

2. **"External reference only" sources (TJA here)** — a source that names a term but doesn't author guidance on it. Currently no field captures this cleanly. Options:
   - (a) Add a top-level `external_references` array (separate from `guidance`) for sources that point elsewhere without issuing their own ruling.
   - (b) Add a `recommendation` enum value like `no-guidance-references-other` and treat as a guidance entry with `quote` being the link context.
   - (a) feels structurally cleaner. **Proposing for v0.3.**

3. **Methodological framing that applies to the term but isn't about the term** (NCDJ, APA, Radical Copyeditor) — no clean slot currently. Three options:
   - Leave out of the term file, capture only in synthesis prose.
   - Add a top-level `methodological_context[]` array that cross-links to framework-level sources.
   - Treat as guidance entries with a new `recommendation: "methodological-framing"` value.

   The Latinx entry solved this ad-hoc: Racial Equity Tools was cited as a guidance entry with `recommendation: "evolving"` even though its quote was meta-framing ("this whole cluster is audience-dependent"). That works, but it conflates "here's where this source lands on the term" with "here's a framework that governs how to think about the term." **Defer to v0.3 after term #3 (Indigenous) likely surfaces this again.**

4. **Sparse coverage** — only 2 direct-guidance sources (SumOfUs, DSG) for a major-cluster term. The schema handles this fine; synthesis just has less to cross-reference. Confirms the schema works for thin terms too. No change needed.

5. **No obvious `related_terms` with their own files** — unlike Latinx (Latine, Latino, Hispanic, Chicanx all warrant separate entries), unhoused/homeless has no semantically-distinct related terms that'd get their own pages. "Unhoused" is an alias of "homeless" for our purposes (they refer to the same people). "Transient," "vagrant," "bum," "indigent," "panhandler" are all avoided terms that live inside this entry, not separate entries. **Schema allows `related_terms: []` — works correctly, but worth flagging that sparse-cluster terms behave this way.**

6. **Approved-terms-to-avoid list** — SumOfUs and DSG both issue lists of avoided terms alongside their preferred term. The schema's per-guidance `quote` + `paraphrase` captures this in prose but doesn't structure the avoided-terms as data. Could add `avoided_terms: [string]` per guidance entry for programmatic rendering. **Defer — not yet a design need.**
