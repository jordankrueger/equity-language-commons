## How to read this

The 664 flags are claims in synthesis/audience_notes prose that the cited
sources don't contain. Three disposition buckets:

- **keep** — legitimate editorial knowledge or corpus-derivable framing; no change
- **verify+cite** — hard falsifiable fact that should carry a citation
- **soften/fix/cut** — stated too flatly, possibly wrong, or stale

## Group rules (proposed)

### 1. "Auditor: excerpt insufficient" (325) — KEEP, skip re-audit

These are claims the auditor couldn't see enough source excerpt to confirm —
a bundle-size artifact, not a verdict. Reading them, nearly all are
**intra-corpus claims** ("APA's term-to-avoid table lists 'addict'",
"Sierra Club and TJA explicitly say…") that Layer 1-style checks against our
own archives could confirm, plus site-structural notes ("this page pairs
with addiction") that aren't factual claims at all.

**Proposal:** keep all; skip the bigger-bundle re-audit pre-launch. The risk
class is low (misdescribing a source we archive locally — readers can check
us) and the verification system already covers quotes and recommendations,
which is where real damage would live.

### 2. Site-structural and corpus-derivable claims (~140 of "Other"/"Dated") — KEEP

Two recurring patterns the auditor can't help flagging:

- Cross-page navigation notes ("the avoid guidance for Oriental lives on its
  own page", "this entry sits beside the chapter's disability terms")
- Chronological corpus framing ("Casey 2013 predates the capitalization
  rule", "SumOfUs predates the post-2020 shift") — this IS the site's
  editorial voice per the no-blame chronological-framing rule; derivable
  from publication dates we hold.

**Proposal:** keep wholesale.

### 3. Sociolinguistic generalizations (~90, mostly "Other") — KEEP where hedged

"Older speakers often prefer American Indian", "Latinx remains the more
common form in US English writing", "younger cohorts skew Latinx/Latine".
This is the synthesis value-add readers come for. Most are properly hedged
(often / tends / many / some) and several actually derive from Pew data
quoted in DSG entries (corpus-supported at one remove).

**Proposal:** keep, except the named items in the exceptions list below
where the hedge is missing or the claim turned prescriptive.

### 4. Hard falsifiable facts (~80 flags → ~25 distinct facts) — VERIFY+CITE

Dates, statutes, statistics, and named events. Deduped, they cluster into
five citation families:

- [ ] **Pew Research** (~12 flags): 2013 Hispanic/Latino preference survey

- [ ] **US Census** (~10 flags): 33.8M multiracial in 2020; 276% growth over

- [ ] **Statutes/legal** (~15 flags): HR 4238 (2016); Title 18 "Indian

**Proposal:** verify each distinct fact against a primary source (WebFetch),

### 5. Exceptions — SOFTEN / FIX / CUT (10 named items)

- [ ] `caucasian` — "Federal demographic categories use Caucasian as a formal
  label." **Likely WRONG** — OMB standards use "White." The Statistics-group
  phrasing ("some older HHS, FBI UCR, and academic survey instruments still
  use Caucasian") is the defensible version. Fix the flat claim to match.
- [ ] `latinx` — "Writers after about 2023 should weight Latine over Latinx
  for Spanish-speaking or bilingual audiences." Prescription exceeds the
  sources; reframe as the TJA-noted trend it derives from.
- [ ] `latine` — "More major guides will likely add Latine entries through
  2025-2026." Prediction, now half-stale (it IS 2026). Cut or convert to a
  dated observation.
- [ ] `negro` — "Negro was the standard, respectful term of the 17th through
  mid-20th centuries." The 17th-century "respectful" framing is shaky;
  soften to the well-supported late-19th-to-mid-20th-century window.
- [ ] `african-american` — "has always carried the same capitalization as
  Asian American, Mexican American…" Drop "always"; parallel-construction
  point stands without the absolute.
- [ ] `homophobia` — "The '-phobia' suffix is criticized in some adjacent
  scholarship." Vague attribution ("some scholarship"). Either cite the
  heterosexism literature or soften to "critics of the suffix argue…"
  framing without the scholarship claim.
- [ ] `dreamer` — "Dreamer polls well precisely because it was coined to."
  Drop "precisely"; the intentional-framing point is fair editorial, the
  certainty isn't.
- [ ] `caucasian` — "Racial-language conventions were generally looser before
  2020." Vague era-claim; harmless but flabby — tighten or cut.
- [ ] `minority` — 2045 projection: hedge as "Census projections from the
  late 2010s" pending the verify pass (see Census family above).
- [ ] `hispanic` — "Hispanic may be received as colder than Latino in these
  communities." Keep only if it stays clearly inside audience_notes voice;
  if it reads as fact, hedge.
