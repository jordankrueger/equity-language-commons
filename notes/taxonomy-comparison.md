## Guide Profiles

### 1. SumOfUs / Hanna Thomas — "A Progressive's Style Guide" (2016)

**Organizing principle:** Issue area (topic-based, social movement framing)
**Depth:** Hierarchical — top-level issue areas, each with subcategories: Resources, Writing Guidelines (framing intro), Specific Recommendations, Terms Used (green column), Terms Avoided/Questioned (orange column)
**Structure:** ~14 issue areas + front matter

**Top-level sections (exact names):**
- Introduction
- Central Principles
  - People-First Language
  - Self-Identification
  - Active Voice
  - Proper Nouns
- Issue Areas:
  - Age
  - Disability
  - Economy
  - Environment/Science
  - Food
  - Gender/Sex
  - Geopolitics
  - Health
  - Housing/Space
  - Immigration/Refugees
  - Indigeneity/Ancestry
  - Police/Incarceration
  - Race/Ethnicity
  - Sexual and Domestic Violence
- Appendix I: Images
- Appendix II: Additional Resources
- Acknowledgements / Endnotes

**Sample terms covered:**
- Age: adolescent, older person, elder abuse, age apartheid / the elderly, geriatric, childish, geezer
- Disability: neurodivergent, PWDs, Deaf culture, substance use / handicapped, crazy, crippled, retarded
- Economy: caste apartheid, people experiencing material poverty, racial equity / culture of poverty, the poor, unskilled labor
- Immigration: asylee, asylum seeker, undocumented / illegal alien, illegal immigrant, anchor baby
- Gender/Sex: (section exists but not fully visible in pages reviewed)

**Notable:** Only guide that covers Food, Geopolitics, Economy, Environment/Science, Health, Housing/Space as top-level categories. Anti-oppression framing language baked into every section ("Anti-ableism framework," "Anti-classist framework," etc.). Explicitly multi-voiced and intersectional by design.

### 3. Trans Journalists Association (TJA) Stylebook (2026-01)

**Organizing principle:** By reporting practice and subject matter (journalism-specific; single-identity deep dive)
**Depth:** Deep hierarchical — sections → subsections → named entries with anchor links. Quarterly updated.
**Structure:** 7 sections, entirely focused on trans coverage

**Top-level sections (exact names):**
- Stylebook and Coverage Guide (intro)
- Editorial Best Practices
  - self-identification is paramount
  - 'trans issues' is a euphemism
  - talk to trans people about trans people
  - talk to trans people about things besides trans people
  - take care not to ask irrelevant or unnecessary questions
  - never out sources
  - there is no single 'trans community'
  - trans terms and identities vary across cultures
  - be aware of slurs and other inflammatory language
  - consult broader industry best practices
- Gender, Name, and Pronoun Usage
  - Gender (don't guess; don't overidentify; past pronouns; "identifies as")
  - Names (deadnames; past coverage errors; web asset errors; when sources deadname)
  - Pronouns (basics; gender-neutral; multiple; neopronouns; preferred; singular they; unknown)
- Politicized or Inaccurate Phrases
  - assigned sex at birth; biological sex; culture war; detransitioner; gender ideology; gender nonbinary; groomer; male-bodied/female-bodied; mutilation/sterilization; opposite sex/gender; rapid-onset gender dysphoria/social contagion; stealth/passing; transgenderism/trans ideology/trans agenda; transgendered; trans activists; TERF/gender-critical; trans-identified; trans male/trans female; woke/wokeness
- Topical Guides
  - Breaking News (identifying trans people; no self-id; trauma-informed; trans children; deaths)
  - Anti-Trans Laws and Policies (general; bathroom restrictions; health care bans; nondiscrimination laws)
  - Criminal Justice (mass shooting misinformation; crimes; incarcerated trans people; incarcerated sources; gender-carceral policy)
  - Health Care (gender-affirming care; gender-neutral terms; gendered medical language; protected health info; detransition/retransition; hormone therapy; reproductive health)
  - Sports (trans people in sports; competitive advantage in youth sports; testosterone regulations; "transgender athletes")
  - Other Guidance (drag; describing trans people's looks; trans history/cultures; obituaries; asylum seekers/refugees; sex work; same-sex marriage; "losing a loved one" cliche; polling data)
- Photography and Visual Media
  - obscure info; informed consent; removing deadnames; file photos
- Glossary of Terms
  - ~35 defined terms: agender, bigender, binary, cis/cisgender, crossdresser, DSD, gender-affirming care, gender binary, gender dysphoria/euphoria, genderfluid, genderqueer, intersex, Latinx/Latine, MtF/FtM, nonbinary, sex, sex change/sex reassignment, top/bottom surgery, trans, transfeminine, transgender, transmedicalism, transition, trans man, transmasculine, transmisogyny, transmisogynoir, transsexual, transvestite, trans woman, Two-Spirit
- Further Reading

**Sample terms (politicized/inaccurate section):**
- groomer, gender ideology, social contagion, mutilation/sterilization, transgenderism, woke — all flagged as politically weaponized; detailed guidance on how/whether to use
- assigned sex at birth, biological sex — nuanced; when appropriate, when not
- detransitioner — use carefully, context-dependent

**Notable:** Journalism-practice framing sets this apart entirely. Criminal Justice and Sports are standalone topical guides with no analog in other guides. "Politicized or Inaccurate Phrases" is a unique category — an explicit misinformation-framing section. Latinx/Latine appears in the glossary but only in relation to trans identity, not as a standalone race/ethnicity entry. Two-Spirit is defined and distinguished from transgender.

### 4. Native Governance Center (NGC) Style Guide (Feb 2021)

**Organizing principle:** Organization-specific + community-specific (relationship-based; single community deep dive)
**Depth:** Flat to shallow — 6 sections, minimal subcategories; more prescriptive rule lists than framing essays
**Structure:** 6 sections, all focused on Indigenous/Native terminology in their organizational context

**Top-level sections (exact names):**
- Why a Style Guide? (intro, cites *Elements of Indigenous Style*)
- Style Guide Navigation (TOC):
  - References to Racial, Ethnic, Cultural (and Political) Groups
  - Our Work
  - The People and Nations We Serve
  - Our Geographic Service Area
  - Using Our Native Languages and Place Names
  - Other Miscellaneous Terminology
  - Inappropriate Terminology

**Content per section:**
- **References to Racial, Ethnic, Cultural (and Political) Groups:** Capitalize Black, Indigenous, Native, American Indian, Asian, Latinx; do NOT capitalize white; use BIPOC over POC when Black/Indigenous are included; use individual identifiers over BIPOC when possible
- **Our Work:** nation rebuilding (not "nation building"); sovereignty/self-determination/self-governance (lowercase); Treaty Right/Treaty Rights (capitalize); Rebuilders program naming conventions
- **The People and Nations We Serve:** Native nations (preferred); Tribal nations/Tribes (secondary); reservation vs. nation distinction; Tribe/Tribal (capitalize regardless of proper noun status); Indian Country (capitalize); Native people/Native/Indigenous; American Indian or Indian when specifically requested
- **Our Geographic Service Area:** Mni Sota Makoce (use alongside "Minnesota"); MN/ND/SD abbreviations acceptable for character limits; "shares geography with" framing for Native nations
- **Using Our Native Languages and Place Names:** do NOT italicize Native-language words; put Native-language word first, English meaning in parentheses (not the reverse)
- **Other Miscellaneous Terminology:** Capitalize Creator, First Nations, Métis, Elder, Sundance, Sweat Lodge, Traditional Knowledge (per *Elements of Indigenous Style*)
- **Inappropriate Terminology:** Washington football team former name (slur); do not say "United States' Native peoples" (implies ownership); do not use "pipeline" metaphor in fundraising context

**Sample terms covered:**
- Preferred: Native nations, Native people, Indigenous, BIPOC, nation rebuilding, Tribal council, Indian Country, Mni Sota Makoce, Elder, Traditional Knowledge
- Avoid: nation building, "United States' Native peoples," "pipeline" (as fundraising metaphor), former Washington football team name

**Notable:** Shallowest structure of the four — no term glossary, no framing essays on -isms. Highly org-specific (references their own programs by name). Introduces *Elements of Indigenous Style* as the upstream authority — NGCs style choices are downstream of that. Language/place names section (no italics for Native words; Native word first) has no equivalent in other guides. "Our Geographic Service Area" section names Mni Sota Makoce — a real-world place-name decolonization example other guides don't model.

## Three Explicit Flags

### 1. Overlap — Where All Four Guides Cover the Same Ground (spine candidates)

These are the only true four-way overlaps:

- **Self-identification principle** — All four assert: ask people how they want to be identified; don't assume. Stated as a core principle. TJA's "self-identification is paramount" = SumOfUs's "Self-Identification" = Sierra Club's "Ask Before Naming Someone's Identity" = NGC's use of individual identifiers over BIPOC umbrella.
- **People-first language** — All four address it. SumOfUs and Sierra Club name it explicitly as a Central Principle / Overall Recommendation. TJA applies it to trans people. NGC applies it to individual tribal identifiers over BIPOC.
- **Capitalization as political act** — All four address capitalization rules, though for different identity terms. SumOfUs (Black, Indigenous); Sierra Club (Black, Tribal, Native, Indigenous; white lowercase); TJA (capitalization in deadname/past-coverage contexts); NGC (most thorough: Black, Indigenous, Native, Elder, Tribal, Traditional Knowledge, Creator, First Nations, Métis).
- **Race/ethnicity terminology** — SumOfUs, Sierra Club, and NGC all cover BIPOC, Black, Indigenous, Latino/Latinx, white. TJA touches Latinx/Latine in its glossary. Three-way solid overlap; TJA is partial.

Near-spine (3 of 4):
- **Disability language** — SumOfUs + Sierra Club (both deep); NGC and TJA silent
- **Immigration terminology** — SumOfUs + Sierra Club (both deep, with SC explicitly citing SumOfUs); TJA (partial, in topical guide); NGC (silent)
- **Visual media guidance** — SumOfUs (appendix) + Sierra Club (full section) + TJA (full section); NGC (silent)

### 2. Divergence — Same Material Organized Differently

**Indigenous: its own category vs. nested under Race**
- SumOfUs: "Indigeneity/Ancestry" — peer issue area alongside "Race/Ethnicity" (separate top-level)
- Sierra Club: "Tribal Sovereignty and Public Lands" — separate from "Racial and Ethnic Identity" (different framing: land/sovereignty, not just identity)
- NGC: entire guide, not nested under race at all — treated as political/legal category, not racial identity
- TJA: subsumes Indigenous under cultural note; Two-Spirit in glossary

**DECISION NEEDED:** Does the unified taxonomy treat Indigenous as a sub-area of Race, or a parallel top-level? NGC's framing suggests parallel (political/sovereign, not racial). Sierra Club frames it under land/environment. SumOfUs treats it as an identity area.

**Housing/Poverty: where does "unhoused/homeless" live?**
- SumOfUs: "Housing/Space" — standalone issue area
- Sierra Club: folded under "Classism and Wealth" (phrases like "at-risk," "the poor" are class language)
- TJA: silent
- NGC: silent

**DECISION NEEDED:** Housing as its own category, or rolled into Class/Economy?

**Gender vs. LGBTQ+ vs. Trans: one bucket or three?**
- SumOfUs: "Gender/Sex" (one area, covers both sex and gender; LGBTQ included)
- Sierra Club: "Gender and Sexuality" (LGBTQI emphasized; population/reproductive rights separate)
- TJA: treats trans identity as the entire subject — not combined with Gender broadly
- NGC: silent

**DECISION NEEDED:** Does the taxonomy have one "Gender/Sex/LGBTQ+" bucket, or separate "Gender & Sexuality" from "Trans Identity"? TJA's depth suggests trans may warrant its own node.

**Economy vs. Labor: same or separate?**
- SumOfUs: "Economy" covers classism + labor/poverty/wages
- Sierra Club: separate "Classism and Wealth" AND "Labor and Workers"
- Others: silent

**DECISION NEEDED:** One "Economy/Class/Labor" area, or split Class from Labor?

**Violence: physical vs. sexual vs. metaphorical**
- SumOfUs: "Sexual and Domestic Violence" — specifically sexual/domestic
- Sierra Club: "Violence and Guns" — about violent metaphors in language, not violence against people as an identity issue; also separate "War and Military Service" for war metaphors
- TJA: Criminal Justice topical guide covers violence involving trans people
- NGC: silent

**DECISION NEEDED:** Does the taxonomy have a Violence category at all? If so, split by type (sexual/domestic violence vs. gun/war metaphors)?

## Candidate Cross-Cutting Tags

These thread through multiple guides but aren't standalone categories:

| Tag | Description | Appears in |
|---|---|---|
| `capitalization-rule` | Whether to capitalize a term (Black, white, Indigenous, Elder, etc.) | All four |
| `people-first-vs-identity-first` | Whether to put person or identity label first (e.g., "autistic person" vs "person with autism") | SumOfUs, Sierra Club |
| `slur-or-reclamation` | Term is a slur, reclaimed slur, or contested between those two | SumOfUs, TJA |
| `contested-term` | Term is used by different communities with different meanings or valence | All four (different terms) |
| `evolving-usage` | Guide explicitly notes the term's guidance is likely to change | SumOfUs, TJA |
| `avoid-as-metaphor` | Term has equity-harmful application when used figuratively (blind, crazy, lame, pipeline) | SumOfUs, Sierra Club, NGC |
| `avoid-for-dehumanization` | Term reduces a person to a status or label | SumOfUs, Sierra Club, TJA |
| `self-id-required` | Always ask before applying this label | All four |
| `org-specific` | Guidance specific to the source org's context, not universal | Sierra Club, NGC (heavily); TJA (lightly) |
| `journalism-specific` | Guidance specific to news reporting context | TJA |
| `sovereignty-framing` | Term touches political/legal sovereignty, not just cultural identity | Sierra Club, NGC |
| `umbrella-vs-specific` | Guide recommends the specific identifier over the umbrella term (BIPOC → specific; Tribes → nation name) | Sierra Club, NGC, TJA |
| `harm-of-euphemism` | Using a softer term obscures the actual harm (e.g., "retirement" of coal plant, "racially charged") | Sierra Club |
