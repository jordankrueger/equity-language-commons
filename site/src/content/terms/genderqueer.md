## Synthesis

**A self-identification term — valid, current, and never assignable from outside.** The three sources agree on the shape of the identity: genderqueer describes people whose gender identity or expression falls outside the man/woman categories — both and neither, fluid between them, or entirely elsewhere (HRC). They equally agree on the rule for writers: the Diversity Style Guide states it should be used "only if someone self-identifies as genderqueer," and warns it is not a synonym for transgender or transsexual. Use-with-care here means exactly that — the care is in attribution, not in the term itself.

The live precision question is genderqueer versus nonbinary. The Trans Journalists Association threads it: the terms are similar but not synonymous, and genderqueer functions as both a specific identity and a broad category. In practice the corpus's nonbinary entries often list genderqueer among related identities, while the genderqueer entries insist on the distinction — a one-way street that itself is the guidance: a writer can note the terms' kinship, but can't swap one for the other on a person's behalf. The word also carries "queer" inside it, with that term's reclamation history; some people embrace genderqueer for precisely that edge, which is one more reason the label travels only by self-identification.

## Audience notes

- **Journalists and editors.** Use the exact word the person uses — genderqueer and nonbinary are not interchangeable, and neither implies transgender. Lowercase; adjective, not noun ("genderqueer people," never "genderqueers").
- **Surveys and forms.** Offer genderqueer and nonbinary as separate options where space allows; collapsing them undercounts both. Free-text self-description remains the most accurate fallback.
