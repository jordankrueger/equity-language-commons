## Synthesis

This is a narrow, practical entry, and the sources agree on it: "accessible" is the preferred word for spaces, facilities, and features designed for disabled access, and it has replaced "handicapped." Sierra Club states the rule directly — avoid "handicapped" for locations or items, and use "accessible parking" and "accessible bathroom stall" instead. NCDJ says the same in its handicap/handicapped entry: while "handicapped parking" survives in laws and regulations, "many prefer the term 'accessible parking.'" The Diversity Style Guide supplies the definition, describing accessibility as the degree to which a facility is "usable and approachable for people with disabilities." APA defines accessibility — by way of the U.S. Office of Civil Rights — as people with disabilities acquiring the same information, interactions, and services as everyone else, "with substantially equivalent ease of use," and ties it to the social model of disability.

The sources differ only in scope. The journalism and movement guides (Sierra Club, NCDJ) treat "accessible" as the settled replacement for "handicapped," with NCDJ's one caveat being that "handicapped" remains acceptable when you are quoting a law, regulation, or official program name that still uses it. APA and DSG supply the concept underneath the usage rule: accessibility as a standard of equal participation.

## Audience notes

- **Event organizers and internal comms.** Use "accessible" for parking, restrooms, entrances, and seating — not "handicapped." When precision helps, name the actual feature ("step-free entrance," "wheelchair-accessible restroom").
- **Journalists.** Keep "handicapped" only when it is the exact legal, regulatory, or program name being cited; otherwise default to "accessible."
