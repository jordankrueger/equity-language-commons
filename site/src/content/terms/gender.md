## Synthesis

**The corpus is unanimous on the core teaching this page exists to carry: gender is not sex.** All three sources draw the same line. NLGJA (2025) calls gender "a social and psychological experience of self, distinct from sex, which refers to biological characteristics," and its separate "sex" entry adds that sex — classifying someone as male, female, or intersex — is "not synonymous with gender." The Trans Journalists Association (entry updated 2023-08-25) says it just as plainly: sex is "a biological condition determined by primary and secondary sex characteristics," and "is not synonymous with gender, which is a social and personal identity." The American Psychological Association (2023) defines gender as a spectrum of masculinity, femininity, gender-expansive, and nonbinary status and contrasts it with sex as biological features, offering the worked example "I am a cisgender woman" versus "I was born a female."

The sources differ mainly in emphasis rather than substance. NLGJA frames the distinction operationally for newsrooms — be precise about whether a story is about sex, gender, or the relationship between them, since "same-sex/different-sex" and "same-gender/different-gender" can mean different things in marriage and relationship coverage. APA frames gender as a spectrum and a sociocultural construction, foregrounding self-description and a global, non-WEIRD-centric view of how gender is conceptualized. TJA keeps its definition compact and routes readers toward "assigned sex at birth," the language it prefers over "biological sex." None of the three treats "gender" as a word to avoid; the recommendation across the set is to use it with care — precisely, and not as a synonym for sex.

Chronologically, these are recent entries (APA 2023; TJA's "sex" entry updated August 2023; NLGJA 2025), and they converge by the time of writing on a settled distinction: sex as a biological classification assigned at birth, gender as a social and personal identity that may or may not align with that assignment. This page is the anchor for that distinction; it pairs with [gender identity](/terms/gender-identity/) (the internal sense of one's own gender), [gender binary](/terms/gender-binary/) (the man/woman framework the sources describe as an oversimplification), and [biological sex](/terms/biological-sex/) (the sex-side term the corpus asks writers to handle carefully).

## Audience notes

See the structured audience notes above: journalists should keep gender (social and personal) and sex (biological) distinct and be precise about which a story addresses; writers covering marriage, relationships, or policy should separate sex assigned at birth, gender identity, and gender expression rather than collapsing them; and advocates should treat gender as a self-identified spectrum, leading with how a person identifies.
