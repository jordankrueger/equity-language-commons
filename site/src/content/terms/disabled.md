## Synthesis

**The corpus has moved from a person-first default to "ask" — both "disabled person" and "person with a disability" are valid, and self-identification governs.** NCDJ is the anchor and the clearest marker of the shift: it explicitly no longer offers a person-first default, because "many people with disabilities take issue with that advice," and instead urges confirming "how people would like to be described" case by case — while noting that identity-first "disabled people" is "widely embraced." GCJT lands at use-with-care, steering away from pity-laden or outmoded terms toward "precise references to specific conditions." SEIU still prescribes the person-first "people with disabilities" and flags the collective noun "the disabled" as the form to avoid.

Two points stay stable across the sources even amid the person-first/identity-first debate: avoid "the disabled" as a collective noun, which erases individuality, and don't treat disabled people as monolithic. What changed is the default — the older blanket person-first rule has given way to "ask first," with identity-first "disabled" now an embraced option rather than an error. This is the general version of the shift visible on [autism](/terms/autism/). Pairs with [disability](/terms/disability/) and [handicapped](/terms/handicapped/).

## Audience notes

- **Journalists and editors.** Ask how a person wants to be described. Avoid "the disabled"; both "disabled person" and "person with a disability" are acceptable depending on preference.
- **Advocates and internal comms.** Don't enforce person-first as the only correct form — identity-first "disabled" is a claimed identity for many.
