## Synthesis

**"Immigrant" is an accepted base term across the corpus** — the disagreement is never about whether to use it, but about precision and relevance. Define American, the chapter's dedicated source, gives it a neutral definition ("a person who migrates to another country, usually for permanent residence"), and Sierra Club uses it freely throughout its guide. The two sources that file it under "use with care," SumOfUs and the Diversity Style Guide, are not rejecting the word; they are guarding against two specific misuses. SumOfUs warns that "immigrant" is frequently misapplied to people born in the country being reported on, and that it has carried negative connotations for years. The Diversity Style Guide applies a relevancy test borrowed from its rule on race: flag someone's immigrant status only when it materially advances the story, and make that relevance explicit.

Where the sources put their real energy is on the modifiers, not the noun. Every guide's central instruction is about what you attach to "immigrant" (never "illegal"; "undocumented" when status is relevant and confirmed) and whether status belongs in the story at all. The Diversity Style Guide adds an operational caution that recurs in this chapter: discuss a source's undocumented status among the source, reporter, and editors before publishing, because disclosure carries deportation risk.

The chronology is one of refinement rather than reversal. SumOfUs (2016) already named the word's baggage and its frequent misapplication; the 2021–2024 guides converge on "use it, but be precise and relevant." Define American's framework is built around distinguishing "immigrant" from migrant, migrant worker, refugee, asylum seeker, and non-immigrant, so writers reach for the accurate term instead of a single flattening label.

## Audience notes

- **Journalists and editors.** Apply a relevancy test: name someone's immigrant or undocumented status only when it advances the story, and raise a source's undocumented status with editors before publishing, given the deportation risk (Diversity Style Guide).
- **Advocates and internal comms.** Center the individual's circumstance over the group label. "Immigrant" can flatten or politicize, so name the specific status or story where you can (SumOfUs, Define American).
- **Precision over catch-all.** Immigrant, migrant, migrant worker, refugee, and asylum seeker are not interchangeable. The accurate term is almost always the better one.
