## Synthesis

**"Autism" / "autism spectrum disorder" is the term to use — and this is one of the clearest cases where identity-first language ("autistic person") is often preferred.** The five sources converge: NCDJ and the Diversity Style Guide both note that "opinions vary," with many autistic people preferring "autistic" as an identity over "person with autism," because the identity-first construction reflects how they understand themselves. Sierra Club generalizes the rule: "as with the Deaf community and autistic community, it is appropriate to use the preferred identity-first language of the community until you can learn an individual's preference." APA and GCJT supply the clinical frame: ASD is "a group of complex neurological conditions," and the Autistic Self Advocacy Network recommends dropping "Asperger's" — folded into ASD in 2013 — in favor of "autism."

So the page carries two settled points and one instruction. Settled: "autism spectrum disorder" is the current clinical umbrella, and "Asperger's" is no longer a separate diagnosis. The instruction: ask, and absent a stated preference, default to the community's identity-first "autistic." That makes autism a key counterexample to a blanket person-first rule — the broader version of the same shift lives on [disabled](/terms/disabled/). Pairs with [disability](/terms/disability/) and [ableism](/terms/ableism/).

## Audience notes

- **Journalists and editors.** Ask. Many autistic people prefer identity-first "autistic"; use "autism spectrum disorder" for the clinical term and drop "Asperger's."
- **Advocates and internal comms.** Don't impose person-first "person with autism" as a default — for much of the community it's the opposite of the respectful choice.
