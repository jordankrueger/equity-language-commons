## What this chapter covers

This chapter gathers the terms source guides use to name people by immigration status: the neutral base term (immigrant), the legally-defined statuses (refugee, and by reference asylum seeker and migrant), the corpus's prescribed term for people without current legal status (undocumented immigrant), and the rejected framings that describe the same population in the language of crime and otherness (illegal immigrant, illegal alien, alien). It also covers the Dreamer / DACA-recipient cluster — the people-terms for undocumented immigrants who arrived as children. Future batches may extend it to related entries such as asylum seeker, migrant, noncitizen, and anchor baby.

**The chapter divides cleanly into prescribe and reject.** Unlike chapters where the hard call is between two defensible forms, here the same group of people draws the corpus's most unanimous endorsement ("undocumented," recommended by every source that addresses it) and its most unanimous rejection ("illegal immigrant" and "alien," avoided by every source that addresses them). The dedicated sources are Define American's media reference guide and the Immigrant Defense Project's journalist guides, the corpus's deepest single treatments of immigration language.

## How sources position themselves

- **Define American — Media Reference Guide (2024)** — the chapter's dedicated source. Defines each status term precisely, lists problematic terms against preferred ones, and frames the whole guide around humanizing immigrants as individuals rather than a monolith.
- **Immigrant Defense Project — Journalist Style Guide (2020) and Comm/Unity Style Guide R4 (2021)** — two IDP guides built around a "problematic terms to reconsider" chart that pairs each rejected term with a person-centered alternative.
- **Sierra Club Equity Language Guide (2021)** — paired "appropriate terms" and "terms to avoid" lists; frames its high standard against the organization's own history of internal anti-immigrant organizing.
- **SumOfUs Progressive Style Guide (2016)** — the earliest source here; supplies the "no person is illegal" argument and the activist-used vs. activist-avoided term columns.
- **Diversity Style Guide (2023)** — peer aggregator; the definitional backbone for the relevancy test, the "never use illegal(s) as a noun" rule, and the February 2021 USCIS shift.
- **Color of Change (2020, antiracist protest reporting)** — people-first language frame; pairs "undocumented person" against "illegal immigrant" alongside its criminal-justice term pairs.
- **Global Center for Journalism & Trauma (2021)** — the legal-precision-plus-humanity treatment of refugee, asylum seeker, migrant, and internally displaced person.
- **Trans Journalists Association (2026, entry updated 2023)** — the self-identification and safety layer for trans and gender-expansive refugees and asylum seekers.

## Chronology

The guidance in this chapter has hardened rather than shifted. The pivot point sits before the corpus's oldest source: the AP Stylebook dropped "illegal immigrant" in 2013, and SumOfUs (2016) onward treat avoidance of "illegal" and "alien" as settled. The 2020–2024 guides refine the prescription — the relevancy test, the deportation-risk caution, the trauma-informed and trans-inclusive layers — rather than reopening the core call. The clearest external marker is institutional: the February 2021 USCIS move away from "alien" and "illegal alien," which the later guides cite as confirmation that the preferred vocabulary now runs through federal practice, not only advocacy. Read the date markers as agreement deepening, not reversing.
